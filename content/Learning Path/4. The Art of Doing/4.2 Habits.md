**Habit** = a behaviour that is repeated consistently, causing it to become subconscious to an extent.

As outlined in the book [Atomic Habits](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits), they require four main components: cue, craving, response and reward. Once this chain is well-established, the habit becomes very easy or even automatic. This is a powerful way to hijack motivation, as a strong, habitual craving beats any motivational quote or video.

Many of the JSM's systems rely on habits to be effective. For example, processes may take some time to follow at first, especially if the steps are complex - but doing so consistently and forming a habit will make you much more efficient, and progress will be made faster. Trackers require a habit of updating them regularly, tools require a habit of using the right ones at the right times, and so on.

In order to build these habits, Atomic Habits suggests four main areas to focus on (based on the four components from earlier):

1: **Make it obvious.** My favourite way to do this is to have a clear to-do list, such as [[Super Productivity|Super Productivity]], so that tasks for the day are unmissable (assuming you can build the habit of constantly checking your to-do list).

2: **Make it attractive.** You need to look forward to it, so make sure it has an enjoyable aspect. Or, make the alternative painful (e.g. an alarm on the other side of the room to make getting out of bed attractive).

3: **Make it easy.** Front-load as much of the work as you can. For example, if you want to cook more, make sure you already have all the ingredients and a recipe ready.

4: **Make it satisfying.** This is especially important at first. I like to heavily reward myself the first few times I do something good (e.g. big meals and late nights after my first few gym sessions ever), and then decrease these rewards as the habit forms to make the activity sustainable. With many skills and activities, having more experience will start allowing you to gain satisfaction from nuances within the activity itself (e.g. hitting new records in gym and seeing physique improvements).
