If you don't sacrifice for what you want, then what you want becomes the sacrifice.

To make big changes, you often need to constrain yourself to a certain way of living. You have to stop doing things you like, start doing things that are boring, and stick to behaviours that feel repetitive. Life is most fun when it's dynamic and exciting, so being limited to fewer options feels inherently bad.

However, a crucial point to understand in any aspect of life is that constraints feel restrictive before they feel freeing. By being forced to live within certain bounds, you can now start to figure out the maximum you can do within them, and you will often realise it's way more than you thought.

For example, for someone on a lean diet, you may limit yourself to a certain number of calories per day. This feels rough at first - calorie counting takes effort, you'll still be hungry in the evenings, and many foods you liked (including 'easy ways out' like supermarket pizzas) will be too inefficient to have anymore. But if you stick with it, little optimisations will start to happen. You'll realise some foods are really satiating but low-calorie. You'll start to make repeatable meals, so you don't have to re-count the calories each time. You'll find strategies to get enough vitamins and protein in your limited quantities. And slowly, you'll start to feel more and more comfortable with your restrictions, until you're eventually spoiled for choice on what healthy, tasty foods to eat - with the added benefit of feeling confident in your choices.

If you are going to impose constraints, follow them passionately. Don't try and dance around your rules. If you're gonna eat clean, don't let yourself go to the supermarket just to browse for a snack that might fit the definition. If you're gonna sleep early, don't wait until the last 5 minutes to start getting ready. Really identify with your constraints. If you're going to break them, do so deliberately and for a good reason.
