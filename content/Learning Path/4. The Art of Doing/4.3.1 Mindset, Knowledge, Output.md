When you want a change to happen in your life, such as a new skill, healthier lifestyle, side project or anything else - results will not be instant. There will be mistakes, it will be difficult to stay consistent, there will be temptations to give up, and so on.

For this reason, immediately chasing output is not a good idea. Instead, I suggest focusing first on mindset, then knowledge, and finally output.

When I say 'mindset', I'm referring to how you think about the change. You can add rules and constraints and promises, but the human brain wants to win whatever game you're playing with it. You will find workarounds and excuses. You need mindset change so that you truly feel aligned with your actions.

For example, let's say you're trying to eat cleanly. You could immediately create a perfect meal rotation, swear to avoid junk food forever, etc. But this is very likely to break, as you'll get withdrawl and go through lots of challenges. A better initial change is to alter how you think about junk food. If you can truly convince yourself that it is poison, increasing your chance of illness, worsening your skin and so on, and you can feel genuinely disgusted when you think about it, then many changes will start to happen naturally. I made avocados the mascot of my clean eating journey, and started obsessing over them. I now have an avocado plush called Avo, a little avocado tree from a seed, an avocado sponge, and avocado emojis on any food-related note. It sounds stupid, but I see it as the ultimate form of mindset alignment, and a constant reminder of what I'm trying to do here. Make it your personality and identity.

Spending some time cultivating the right mindset is very valuable, but it won't actually make progress. So there's a balance to strike between thinking and preparing vs actually doing things. Even if it seems like you're behind the curve, spending time putting yourself in the right headspace first can allow you to make progress much faster and more consistently than others, and you can prevail.

When you do take action, be prepared to fail and learn. You have to do things the wrong way for a while before you can understand the value of the right way. The important thing is to gain and document knowledge from this stage. If you fail with the right mindset and you gain knowledge from it, then that's actually a great success, even if the output doesn't show it yet.

Don't try to convince yourself that your output is high during the knowledge phase. Many ideas are likely to fail. If you start telling people and bragging about your new approaches and solutions, it will make it awkward when you have to explain that all of them didn't really work out. Support is incredibly useful, but embrace the fact that you're new to this, and that you're testing everything without caring about whether you succeed or fail yet. Your output will eventually speak much louder than your words do now.

Finally, when you feel as though you have some strategies that work well, you can focus on output. Make sure this does happen - don't experiment forever. As soon as you feel like your learning starts to plateau, you should see your output skyrocket. If it doesn't, then why? Don't let yourself be a hypocrite who THINKS right and KNOWS right, but doesn't DO right. [[4.2 Habits|Habits]] will help.

So, when you go to a JSM system group to optimise a certain topic, start with the mindsets. Put your mind in the right place, so that your body can naturally follow. Then, refer to knowledge as you make mistakes and learn more. Only after this will the true value of the other systems really shine.
