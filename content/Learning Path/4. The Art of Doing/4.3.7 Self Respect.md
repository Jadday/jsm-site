Other people are great. There are so many amazing things they can teach you, give you and do for you. Building genuine relationships feels amazing, and many of the best times in life occur with others.

However, this requires a level of trust, which could always be broken. The more trust and respect you put into someone, the more it would hurt to feel betrayed by them. And the more dependence you build on them, the worse off you'll be if they suddenly disappear.

The solution here is not detachment or isolation, it is just regulation of how you feel about yourself and others.

You have to remember that you are a person. Although you control your own movements and words, you can still engage with yourself, learn about yourself and work with yourself. Something that I feel is too rare nowadays is the ability to see yourself as a friend. Someone who makes you laugh, who you can talk through ideas with, go on adventures with, etc. It's spending time WITH yourself, not BY yourself. There's a human right there, even if you need a mirror to look at them. You are never alone.

If you can truly see yourself as a friend, you immediately have someone to fall back on even if every other social relationship goes wrong. However, you can also break your own trust. Having unplanned exceptions, acting in misalignment with your beliefs, and failing to meet commitments can all make you think lower of yourself and struggle in your own company. You also need self-respect.

When you really respect someone, you will treat them well. You will put effort into keeping them happy and healthy, you will buy things for them, compliment them, etc. Self-respect is no different. It's not about trying to just appear happy and healthy for others. It's how you behave when there's no one else around, just you and yourself - do you eat lower quality food? Do you binge watch content? Do you pick your spots, skip gym, sleep late? And so on. If any of the answers are yes, but you'd fix those things if others were around, then you need to remember the value of your own company. Respect the person you're building.

Another aspect of self-respect is how you interact with other people, especially ones you like. It's easy to feel like you need to be valuable and useful to make them like you, but this isn't true, and can lead to you chasing them unnecessarily. People aren't really drawn to "someone who's attached to them", they're drawn to people with similar interests, cool personalities, funny jokes, attractive features, whatever. Rather than working to obsess over one specific person, work to appreciate and improve your own self-value.

If you feel like you owe everything to everyone, and you chase them by default, your standards will appear low. You are allowed to have high standards, but you have to justify them - to yourself and to others. Respect, value and improve yourself without becoming an egoist, and then other people will respect you too.
