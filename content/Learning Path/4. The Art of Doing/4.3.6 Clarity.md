Clarity is extremely useful. Every decision feels better when it's clear, as you can be confident in your choices.

Sometimes, when there are lots of options, things can get messy. You can feel like you're approaching it wrong, or missing something. Clarity often comes from knowing or being told the best decision to make at that moment. Much of this comes from external structure - a plan, rule, guide, even an idol telling you what to do.

For example, if you are given a book on music theory, with structured chapters, exercises and tests, you can instantly feel confident in that structure. Even though there's nothing holding you to it, the fact that it gives you a clear next step every time makes it incredibly valuable.

But when external structure isn't available, and you have to make a decision, the only real strategy is to do what feels right. Forget the millions of variables that might influence things. One option feels right in your gut. Do it, regardless of the trade-offs.
