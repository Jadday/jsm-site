A classic saying is "work smart not hard", and this concept really can benefit any topic if it's applied correctly.

A lot of classic motivational advice tells you to push through, work hard, and learn to enjoy suffering. Some advice I received in a work email contained the following:

"Don't believe in luck, believe in hard work. Stop trying to rush the process or search for a shortcut. There are none."

"There will be days when you don't feel like doing it, but you must push through those days regardless of how you feel."

My issue with these quotes is that they come across quite harsh and demanding. This is quite far from self-respect, it's almost treating yourself like a machine. I believe that life shouldn't have to be hard if you manage it correctly.

For example, I want to do many different projects in life. Work, JSM, gamedev, music production, guitar, art, etc. I always struggled with balancing these, as I'd forget about some of them for long periods, and my progress was very patchy. I was convinced that the issue was effort - I just needed to work harder and do more. However, after reading Super Productivity's [side project guide](https://super-productivity.com/blog/finish-side-projects-developer-guide/), I made the change of "ruthlessly" only doing one side project at a time, making it so much easier to make focused, consistent progress. Immediately, my output skyrocketed, despite me putting in the exact same amount of effort.

Changing your strategy is much more effective than changing your operations. If something is consistently difficult, don't assume you're lazy - just try to create or research a new approach to it.
