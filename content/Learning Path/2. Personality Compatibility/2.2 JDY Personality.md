This note contains a set of my own strengths and weaknesses, which I recently identified by completing and writing about the psychometric tests outlined in [[2.1 Assessment]].

The purpose of showing these here is to demonstrate the level of openness and detail to use when writing your own characteristics.
## Strengths

I am very good at planning and organising. I will set my direction in life, and plan how to get there all the way down to individual tasks, and I will keep all of this organised.

Within a focused period, I will produce very impressive work at a blazing speed. This means I can get by at only an hour or two of deep work a day, and doing more puts me ahead of the game.

Doing things for or with another person I like provides great motivation for me. Impressing someone is a big enough reward for my brain to get going. I'm also likely to respond yes to productive invites, such as study sessions.

My negative emotions, such as anger and jealousy, rarely manifest externally. Instead, I turn them into motivation internally to be better and prove myself, which can make me productive rather than destructive.

I have a very vivid imagination, with new ideas coming to me regularly and appearing clear as day in my mind. I have a great vision of what the end goal should look like, and I can play through situations in my head to think about them in more detail.

I stick well to progress that has structure. Courses, learning plans and to-do lists all provide a clear definition of success, which is progressing forward and eventually finishing or passing the resource. If I can find this scaffolding, I mold well to it and have a lot more trust in what I'm doing.

I abide strongly to external laws and rules in place. I'm much less likely to break a rule if it feels externally imposed. This can be a really powerful source of motivation if some external force is driving me to complete a task, as it feels non-negotiable.

When it comes to the right activity - something that I'm enjoying, is using my brain a little but not too challenging or stressful, and I seem to be making good progress - I can work like a machine. I can do hours without a break, and still be hungry for more.

I can sometimes get random sparks of motivation to be productive, usually coming from envisioning a cool idea, and these can inspire work when I otherwise might have felt quite demotivated.

I'm not attached to how I currently am, and I'm very open to change. I will happily embrace large productivity and lifestyle changes to experiment with them, meaning I don't get 'stuck in my ways'.
## Weaknesses

I don't have a great short-term memory, so I can forget about a lot of the structure that I planned and architected into place, like best practices, defined processes, frequent realignment etc.

I procrastinate starting on tasks that are boring, as I know I don't want to do them, and I feel a strong boredom wall. When I force myself to do them, I'm very prone to environmental and internal distraction.

I procrastinate tasks that are stressful, as I know I'll have to make decisions that could go wrong.

I procrastinate tasks that are difficult, as I'm anticipating lots of tiring work, and it just makes my brain feel instantly tired and drained just to think of them.

I am prone to getting a sort of brain fog, where I know exactly what I have to do, it's not particularly difficult or boring or stressful, it's just effort, and I'm fully equipped with time and energy to do it - but somehow I just feel like I can't, and I typically fall to media as an easy way out. I feel heavy and slow, and time passes quickly.

I am too optimistic with time estimation for daily tasks. I imagine the best possible scenario and schedule as if it is guaranteed to happen.

Deadlines that are far away feel non-existent to me, so I'm unlikely to care about working on them until they're close and feel urgent. Without the pressure, I'm much less bothered.

For things that I don't love doing, I will typically complete one focus session and then tell myself "I've done enough of that for today". This means I don't make much progress in these things, even if I have the spare time to.

Once I do get into a flow with something, I can spend much longer than anticipated on it, sometimes with a dropoff in output that means a lot of the work was inefficient. If there's no timer in place, this can take up a significant chunk of a day.

My energy pattern is seemingly quite inconvenient. Assuming no huge distractions, I have the most energy in the early morning, which then starts to drop off, hitting a low in the afternoon. This makes it quite low for most of the typical work day. Then, it surges back up in the evening, making it difficult to sleep early.
