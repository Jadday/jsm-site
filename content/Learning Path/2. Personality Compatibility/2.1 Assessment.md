No approach to life will work for everyone, and no one solution will work for everything. The JSM represents just one approach, so it may work well for some people, but not for others.

However, this distinction isn't random. Different thinking styles and personalities benefit from different methods. As the JSM is crafted to work specifically for me, people with a similar way of thinking are more likely to benefit from it.

To assess how well the JSM might work for someone, I performed some psychometric tests. My results show why an approach like the JSM works well for me, and someone who has similar findings would likely benefit in similar ways.

The tests used are detailed below:
1. Big Five Personality Test (available [here](https://openpsychometrics.org/tests/IPIP-BFFM/))
2. Executive Function (available [here](https://novopsych.com/assessments/formulation/executive-skills-questionnaire-revised-esq-r/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
3. Morningness/Eveningness Questionnaire (available [here](https://qxmd.com/calculate/calculator_829/morningness-eveningness-questionnaire-meq))
4. 6-Question ADHD Screening (available [here](https://add.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/adhd-questionnaire-ASRS111.pdf))
5. Pure Procrastination Scale (available [here](https://psytests.org/emvol/pcppsen-run.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The way that you approach these tests is important. Rather than just looking for interesting results like "84% introverted", use your answers and results to actually write about how they apply to you, describing everything you feel about them in detail. Then, look at your findings and create a set of your strengths and weaknesses. You can compare these to mine, which are documented in [[2.2 JDY Personality]].

After completing the tests and having documentation explaining the results, you can check if your traits align with the JSM in [[2.3 Compatibility]].
