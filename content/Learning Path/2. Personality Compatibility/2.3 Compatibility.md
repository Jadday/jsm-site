As shown in [this paper](https://www.prolificpersonalities.com/prolific-personalities-research-paper.pdf), different productivity systems and frameworks work better for different types of people with specific 'personality profiles'.

As one such framework, the JSM is likely to work much better for people with certain characteristics. This JSM-aligned personality is centred around vast future plans, but poor daily execution.

A JSM-aligned personality enjoys long-term planning, and is very much forward-thinking. They have extensive visions for the future, and they want to change as a person, fixing problems in their life and achieving their goals. They enjoy structured paths towards these goals, being able to tick off milestones and see progress at each stage. They abide well to externally-imposed rules and deadlines, such as laws and work due dates.

There are also several flaws that work well with the JSM, as it can provide scaffolding for them. A JSM-aligned personality has a poor short-term memory, often forgetting the plans they had or the rules and constraints they put in place. They naturally struggle with discipline and persistence, procrastinating tasks that are difficult, boring or stressful. And they are prone to anxiety and worry, especially when there is lots of work piled up and it feels overwhelming.

If these characteristics do not match you at all, then the JSM is unlikely to work well, though some assorted advice from it will likely still be useful. However, in this situation, I urge you to create a personalised productivity system of your own. Replace the J in JSM with your own initial, and create infrastructure that harnesses your specific strengths and mitigates your flaws. You can use the JSM as an example. This concept, which I'm naming "The XSM" for now, is something that I'll try to write a full book about someday.
